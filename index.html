Why is this money sitting there?

If a home owed $50,000 in taxes but sold at a county auction for $150,000, the remaining $100,000 is called a “surplus.” By law, this money belongs to the previous homeowner. In 2023 the U.S. Supreme Court confirmed in Tyler v. Hennepin County that a government cannot keep more than it is owed.

But counties do not actively search for you. And if a claim isn’t filed correctly and on time, the money can go to other lienholders or revert to the county.

Questions people ask us

Is this a scam?

It’s a fair question. You should never pay upfront to recover money that’s yours. We don’t. You can look up your case on your county clerk’s website yourself, and we’ll show you the case number so you can.

Can I claim it myself?

Yes. Surplus claims are public processes and you can file without us. Many people use a service because the filings, deadlines, and hearings can be complicated, especially when other lienholders compete for the same funds.

How long does it take?

It depends on the county and whether the claim is contested. Uncontested claims commonly resolve in a few months.

What happens to my personal information?

We use it only to search for and file your claim. We never sell it.
